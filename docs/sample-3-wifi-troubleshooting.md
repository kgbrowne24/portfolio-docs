## About This Sample

**Objective:** Demonstrate how to create structured, user-focused troubleshooting documentation that helps users independently diagnose and resolve common technical issues.

**Approach:** Developed a knowledge base article addressing common Wi-Fi connectivity problems. Organized the content around observable symptoms and progressively introduced troubleshooting steps, beginning with simple checks before moving to more advanced solutions.

**Key documentation decisions:**
- Used symptom-based headings to help users quickly identify relevant information.
- Organized troubleshooting steps from least disruptive to more advanced actions.
- Provided clear, sequential instructions with expected results where appropriate.
- Used accessible language to support users with varying levels of technical experience.
- Included escalation guidance for issues that cannot be resolved through self-service.

**Skills demonstrated:** Troubleshooting documentation, knowledge base development, technical support content, task-based writing, and user experience.

**Audience:** Home users with no networking background <br />
**Goal:** Help readers solve common problems on their own, in clear, short steps

## Wi-Fi is connected but there is no internet

**Symptoms:** Your device shows it's connected to Wi-Fi, but websites and apps won't load.

**Try this:**

1. Check another device. If it also has no internet, the problem is your network, not your device.
2. Unplug the power from your modem and your router. Wait 30 seconds.
3. Plug in the modem first and wait about two minutes for its lights to settle.
4. Plug in the router and wait another two minutes.
5. Reconnect your device and try loading a website.

**Still not working?** Check your internet provider's website or app for an outage in your area. If there isn't one, contact your provider and tell them you've already restarted your modem and router.

## Wi-Fi is slow in one room

**Symptoms:** Internet works well near your router but slows down or drops in other rooms.

**Why it happens:** Wi-Fi signals get weaker with distance and when they pass through walls, floors, and large objects.

**Try this:**

1. Move closer to the router and run a speed test. If speed improves, the signal is the issue.
2. Place the router in a central, open spot, off the floor and away from metal objects, thick walls, and microwave ovens.
3. If your router offers two bands, try the 5 GHz network. It's usually faster but covers less distance than 2.4 GHz.
4. Restart the router.

**Still not working?** A mesh system or Wi-Fi extender can help in larger homes. Contact your provider or the router's manufacturer for recommendations.

## I forgot my Wi-Fi password

**Try this:**

1. Check the label on the bottom or back of your router. If you never changed the default password, it's printed there.
2. If you have a device that's already connected, look in its Wi-Fi settings for an option to show the saved password.
3. Sign in to your router's settings page, using the address and login printed on the router's label, and look for the Wi-Fi settings.

**Last resort:** A factory reset restores the default password, but it also erases all your router settings. Use it only if the other steps don't work.
